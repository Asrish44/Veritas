# Biomarker-Guided Immunotherapy in Metastatic Melanoma: A Randomized Phase-II Clinical Trial

## Authors & Clinical Affiliations
Dr. Elena Rostova, MD, PhD¹, Dr. Marcus Vance, MD²
¹Department of Oncology, University Medical Center, Zurich

## Clinical Trial Registration
ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05928192

## Abstract
**Background**: Resistance to immune checkpoint blockade remains a formidable barrier in advanced melanoma. We investigated whether baseline circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) biomarker quantification predicts response to dual PD-1/CTLA-4 inhibition.
**Methods**: A randomized, prospective, multi-center Phase-II trial (N=240 patients with unresectable stage IV melanoma) was conducted across 6 academic hospital networks. Patients were stratified by high vs. low baseline ctDNA variant allele frequency (VAF) and allocated to biomarker-adapted dosing vs. standardized dosing.
**Results**: The primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) at 12 months was 62.4% in the biomarker-guided group compared to 41.8% in the control arm (Hazard Ratio = 0.58; 95% CI, 0.42 to 0.79; p < 0.001). Treatment-related grade 3/4 adverse events occurred in 28.3% of the biomarker group versus 39.2% of the standard group.
**Conclusions**: Liquid biopsy ctDNA biomarker stratification substantially improves therapeutic response while diminishing systemic toxicity.

## 1. Introduction
Metastatic melanoma prognosis has improved following the introduction of anti-PD-1 (nivolumab, pembrolizumab) and anti-CTLA-4 (ipilimumab) monoclonal antibodies. Nonetheless, approximately 40% to 50% of patients exhibit primary non-responsiveness or early recurrence. There is an urgent clinical imperative for predictive molecular biomarkers that enable precision dose calibration.

## 2. Patients and Methods
### 2.1 Study Design & Ethical Approval
This study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Review Boards (IRB-2023-098) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All participating patients signed informed consent before enrollment.

### 2.2 Molecular Assay Protocols
Peripheral blood (10 mL) was harvested into cell-free DNA BCT tubes. Plasma ctDNA was isolated using automated magnetic bead extraction, followed by targeted next-generation sequencing (NGS) utilizing a 68-gene oncology panel.

### 2.3 Statistical Design & Endpoints
The study was powered at 90% power with a two-sided alpha of 0.05. Survival distributions were estimated via Kaplan-Meier curves and compared using the log-rank test. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for age, ECOG performance status, and LDH levels.

## 3. Results
- **Patient Cohort**: 240 patients were randomized (120 biomarker-guided, 120 standard). Median age was 58.4 years; 58% were male.
- **Efficacy Outcome**: Median overall survival was not reached in the biomarker arm versus 18.6 months in the standard arm (p = 0.002).
- **Safety Profile**: Colitis (8.3%) and endocrinopathies (12.5%) were the most prevalent immune-related adverse events.

## 4. Discussion
These results demonstrate that dynamic ctDNA monitoring allows early identification of responders and non-responders, mitigating hyper-progression risk. Biomarker-steered dosage intensification delivers superior efficacy while preserving quality of life.
